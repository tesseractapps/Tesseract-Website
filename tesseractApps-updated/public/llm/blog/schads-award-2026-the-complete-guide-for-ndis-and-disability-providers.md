# SCHADS Award 2026: The Complete Guide for NDIS and Disability Providers 

SCHADS Award rates rose 4.75% from 1 July 2026. Plain-English guide to pay rates, classifications, penalties and allowances for NDIS and disability providers. 

Most operations managers know the SCHADS Award exists. Fewer know it well enough to catch a classification error before it becomes a backpay liability. The Fair Work Commission confirmed a 4.75% increase to all SCHADS Award minimum pay rates on 2 June 2026, effective from the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026, not from 1 July itself. That distinction catches providers out every year.

This guide covers the award in plain English: what it includes, how SCHADS classifications work, the updated pay rates, and what the 2026 changes mean for NDIS workforce compliance.

## What is the SCHADS Award? 

The SCHADS Award, formally the Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services Industry Award 2010 (MA000100), sets legally enforceable minimum wages and conditions for more than 250,000 workers across disability, home care, aged care and community services in Australia. It is made by the Fair Work Commission and enforced by the Fair Work Ombudsman.

People still call it the social and community services award, which is actually one of four streams inside it.

For an NDIS provider, the award almost certainly covers your support workers, personal carers, community access staff, support coordinators, recovery coaches and case managers. It is also, without much competition, one of the most complex awards in the country. Eight classification levels, four streams, multiple pay points within each level, and penalty rate, [broken shift](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-broken-shifts-rostering-without-breaches), [sleepover ](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-sleepover-active-hours-travel)and travel allowance provisions that interact differently depending on the type of work being performed. Getting one thing wrong means getting it wrong every fortnight.



## What changed for 2026 and why it matters more than usual ?

The headline is 4.75%. Every SCHADS minimum rate goes up by that figure from the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026. Last year's increase was 3.5%, so this year's jump is steeper.

On the same day, the Superannuation Guarantee rate moves to 12%. Your actual on-cost per worker rises by more than the wage number alone suggests. If you are running thin margins between what NDIS price limits allow and what it costs to deliver a shift, July 2026 is a month to model carefully.

Two other changes landed around the same time. From 1 June 2026, following the Fair Work Commission's decision in [2025] FWCFB 292, work performed immediately before and after a sleepover is treated as a single continuous shift for overtime purposes. Payroll settings and written sleepover agreements needed updating before that date. If you also deliver aged care, watch 1 August 2026, when home care employees under Schedule E may move again under the aged care Work Value case. That second date is easy to miss.

## SCHADS classifications: where most underpayments actually start? 

Here is the thing about SCHADS classifications that does not get said enough: the level is set by what a worker does, not what their contract says or what qualification they hold. A worker with a degree performing Level 2 duties is paid at Level 2. A Certificate III holder performing Level 3 duties is entitled to Level 3 pay. The Fair Work Ombudsman's compliance activity consistently shows misclassification, usually downward, as one of the most common underpayment causes in the sector.

A worker's classification is determined by three inputs: qualifications (Certificate III, Certificate IV, diploma or degree), the duties they actually perform day to day, and their experience. Duties carry the most weight.

The award runs across four streams. The Social and Community Services (SACS) stream covers case management, support coordination, recovery coaching and community development. It is the most common stream for NDIS coordination roles, and the one most often misapplied to direct support workers. The Disability Services stream covers direct support work, personal care and community access. If your support workers are delivering NDIS funded services in participants' homes or the community, this is almost certainly their stream, not SACS. The Home Care stream covers home care workers and domestic assistants, and Crisis Accommodation covers refuge and crisis support roles.

Within each level sit pay points that workers progress through over time. A Level 3 at pay point 1 and a Level 3 at pay point 3 are meaningfully different wages. Misread the stream or the pay point and the error runs on every single pay cycle.

## SCHADS pay rates 2026 

The table below shows indicative full-time and part-time hourly rates for the SACS stream after the 4.75% increase. Use them for budgeting, and confirm exact figures against the Fair Work Ombudsman's MA000100 pay guide once updated for the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026.

Under the indicative 2026 SCHADS Award hourly rates, **Level 2 employees are paid around $36.20 per hour, Level 3 employees around $40.50 per hour, and Level 4 employees around $46.70 per hour at Pay Point 1.**

Casual employees receive a 25% loading on top of the base rate in place of paid leave entitlements. That loading does not stack on top of overtime rates. Do not apply SACS rates to Disability Services or Home Care workers. The streams have different tables, and conflating them is a common source of error.

## Penalties and allowances: the part that surprises most managers 

Base rates are the floor. What actually gets paid on any given shift depends on when it falls, how it is structured, and what the worker does during it.

[Penalty rates apply outside ordinary hours](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-overtime-penalty-rates), broadly Monday to Friday between 6am and 8pm, escalating on evenings, nights, Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. [Overtime works differently by stream](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-overtime-penalty-rates): SACS and crisis accommodation employees are on 150% for the first three hours and 200% beyond that, while disability services and home care employees move to 200% after just two hours. Sunday overtime is double time. [Public holiday overtime](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-overtime-penalty-rates) is double time and a half.

Allowances catch a lot of providers out. [Broken shift allowances apply](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-broken-shifts-rostering-without-breaches) when a shift contains an unpaid break. [Sleepovers carry a specific allowance](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-sleepover-active-hours-travel) of 4.9% of the standard rate for each continuous eight-hour sleepover period, plus overtime rates for any active work overnight. Staff driving between clients in their own car are entitled to a per-kilometre vehicle allowance. There are also [minimum engagement rules](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-minimum-engagement): SACS employees must be paid for a [minimum of three hours per shift](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-minimum-engagement), most others for two. A worker who does 90 minutes is still owed the three-hour minimum on the SACS stream.

### Worked example: what a Saturday visit actually costs 

Take a permanent Level 2 SACS worker on an indicative base of around $36.20 an hour. Saturday work attracts a penalty lifting the rate to roughly $54.30 before allowances. Apply the three-hour minimum engagement and a 90-minute visit costs around $163 in wages alone, before super, travel or admin. That is the gap between the NDIS weekend price limit and your delivery cost, and it is why shifting non-urgent supports to weekdays is one of the more practical ways to protect a participant's budget and your margin at the same time.

## SCHADS and NDIS pricing: the link providers often underestimate 

The NDIA's Disability Support Worker Cost Model sits behind the [NDIS Pricing Arrangements](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/ndis-pricing-arrangements-2026-27) and is built on SCHADS rates. When SCHADS goes up, your cost of delivering supports rises from 1 July. The price limit you can claim only adjusts on the NDIS pricing cycle, which does not always align. In that gap, you absorb the difference. Add super moving to 12% on the same day and the margin compression is real. In a sector running on tight unit economics, the July changeover warrants proper financial modelling, not just a payroll system update.

The compliance exposure runs the other direction too. [Underpayments under the Fair Work Act](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-breaches-fair-work-software-prevention) can be backdated, the Ombudsman can investigate, and [NDIS quality audits](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-audit-guide-self-assess-fair-work) increasingly check that [rostering records, timesheets, payroll and claiming data](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/how-ndis-providers-track-employee-hours-accurately) all reconcile. [Auditors ask how SCHADS is interpreted](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-audit-guide-self-assess-fair-work) and whether that interpretation is documented. "It's in our payroll system somewhere" does not satisfy an auditor.

## What to actually do before 1 July ?

Four things, in order. Audit every worker classification against current duties, not the duties listed when they were onboarded. Roles drift. Classifications often do not. Update your payroll system for the 4.75% wage increase and the 12% super rate, both from the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026. Check that rostering surfaces penalty rates, broken shift allowances, minimum engagement and overtime before a shift is published, not in the payroll run two weeks later. And make sure your [rostering and payroll systems](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/how-ndis-providers-track-employee-hours-accurately) actually talk to each other, because re-keying hours between two systems is where rates go stale and errors propagate.
