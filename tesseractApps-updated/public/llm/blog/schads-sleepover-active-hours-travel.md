# Sleepover Allowance, Active Hours and Travel Time: SCHADS Made Simple 

Sleepovers, active hours and client travel under SCHADS — how to calculate, code and pay correctly in Australian SIL and in-home services. 

Three provisions sit at the heart of almost every SIL and in-home support roster under the [SCHADS Award](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-2026-the-complete-guide-for-ndis-and-disability-providers), and three provisions are where the payroll errors concentrate. Sleepovers, active overnight hours and travel time between clients are each governed by specific clauses in the award, each has its own rate, and each interacts with the others in ways that a flat hourly rate calculation will never capture. If your organisation delivers overnight supports, Supported Independent Living or multiple home visits in a single day, this is the part of the award that will determine whether your payroll is compliant.

This guide covers all three: what the award says, how the 2026 rates work, and what the June sleepover variation means for your SIL payroll now.

## Sleepovers under SCHADS: what the award actually says? 

A sleepover under clause 25.7 of the SCHADS Award is a specific type of overnight arrangement where an employee is required by the employer to remain at the workplace or participant's home overnight, is permitted to sleep, and is available to assist if needed. It is not a shift. It is not a rest break. And it is not the same as an active overnight shift where the worker is awake and working throughout.

Getting that distinction right matters because the two are paid completely differently.

A sleepover attracts a flat allowance of 4.9% of the standard rate per continuous 8-hour sleepover period. The standard rate is the minimum pay for a SCHADS Social and Community Services employee at Level 3 Pay Point 3. From 1 July 2026, that works out to approximately $62.87 per sleepover night, after the 4.75% annual wage review increase. That allowance covers the entire sleepover period. The worker is not paid an hourly rate for the hours they are asleep.

There are two other requirements that attach to every legitimate sleepover. First, at least one of the periods of work immediately before or immediately after the sleepover must be rostered or paid as a minimum of four hours. A sleepover that floats in isolation with a 30-minute handover on each side does not meet this requirement. Second, if the employee is woken during the sleepover to perform work, that call-to-duty is paid at overtime rates with a minimum payment of one hour per call-out, regardless of how brief the actual intervention was.

## What changed on 1 June 2026?

FWC decision [2025] FWCFB 292, handed down in December 2025, took effect from the first full pay period on or after 1 June 2026. Two things changed that every SIL manager needs to understand.

The work immediately before and immediately after a sleepover is now treated as a single continuous shift for overtime purposes. Under the previous interpretation, a six-hour evening block and a four-hour morning block could be read as two separate shifts. From 1 June 2026 they are one shift with ten combined hours of active work, assessed as a single engagement against overtime thresholds.

A sleepover also no longer counts as a rest break between the work periods that surround it. You cannot treat the sleepover as satisfying the rest requirement between the evening and morning blocks. The rest entitlement sits separately and still needs to be met.

One thing that did not change: the 15% night shift loading does not apply to work simply because it adjoins a sleepover. Confirmed by the Full Federal Court in [2026] FCAFC 25. Work on each side of the sleepover is assessed on its own time-of-day profile.

## Active overnight hours: a completely different calculation 

An active overnight shift is one where the employee is awake and working throughout. There is no sleepover allowance. There is no passive period. The worker performs care, supervision or other duties continuously, and every hour is paid at the applicable time-of-day and day-of-week penalty rate.

The cost difference between the two is significant. A sleepover with no call-to-duty costs the provider $62.87 in allowance plus adjacent shift hours. An active overnight shift from 10pm to 6am for a Level 2 SACS worker attracts night penalty rates on every hour, which is why some providers try to roster what is functionally an active night as a sleepover. The Fair Work Ombudsman is alert to this. The test is whether the employee is genuinely permitted to sleep and only required to provide assistance if needed. A worker who must maintain awareness and respond frequently throughout the night is not on a sleepover, regardless of what the roster says.

## Travel time between clients: what is paid and what is not?

Travel time under the SCHADS Award is governed by clauses 20.2 and 20.3. Travel between clients during a shift is paid at the worker's ordinary rate and counts toward total hours worked, minimum engagement and overtime thresholds. Travel from the worker's home to the first client, and from the last client home, is not paid.

When a worker uses their own vehicle between clients or to transport a participant, a vehicle allowance applies. From 1 July 2026, that rate is $1.01 per kilometre under clause 20.3, up from $0.99/km in 2025-26. It covers fuel, depreciation and running costs. Reasonable parking and tolls during the shift can be reimbursed separately. Workers should record start and finish addresses and total kilometres for the day. The allowance applies equally when driving a participant to an appointment, not just when travelling empty between locations.

## How the three provisions interact in a SIL roster?

Take a support worker in a SIL house who works the following pattern on a Wednesday:

- 3pm to 9pm: active support (six hours)
- 9pm to 7am: sleepover (ten hours, permitted to sleep)
- 7am to 11am: morning support (four hours)

From 1 June 2026, the 3pm to 9pm shift and the 7am to 11am shift are treated as a single continuous shift of ten active hours. Overtime thresholds are assessed on the combined ten hours, not on each block separately. The sleepover period from 9pm to 7am is paid at the flat $62.87 allowance. If the worker is woken at 2am to assist a participant, that intervention is paid at overtime rates with a one-hour minimum.

By agreement, a shift including a sleepover can have up to 12 hours of active work, with a cap of eight ordinary hours on each side. Six hours before and four hours after sits within that cap, so no overtime applies on the active hours alone, though stream-specific thresholds still need checking. If the same worker travelled from a previous client to the SIL house at 2:45pm, those travel minutes are paid work time and the kilometres attract the $1.01/km vehicle allowance. The drive from their home to the previous client is not paid.

## Common mistakes in SIL and overnight payroll 

Treating the sleepover as a rest break between shifts is now a breach since 1 June 2026. Providers relying on the sleepover to satisfy the rest requirement between evening and morning blocks need to review their SIL rosters.

Not meeting the four-hour minimum on either side is another common gap. The award requires at least one adjacent work period of four hours. A two-hour handover on both sides does not satisfy this.

Calculating the call-to-duty minimum incorrectly is a recurring error. A 15-minute bed repositioning at 3am is still paid as one hour at overtime rates. The five-minute actual duration is irrelevant.

On travel, the most common mistake is not capturing it. Workers who do not record travel time and kilometres between clients means the entitlement never reaches payroll, producing an underpayment on every shift involving client-to-client travel.

## Sleepover, active hours and travel FAQ 

### What is the SCHADS sleepover allowance in 2026? 

Approximately $62.87 per sleepover night from 1 July 2026, reflecting the 4.75% annual wage review increase. It is 4.9% of the standard rate (SACS Level 3 Pay Point 3) under clause 25.7(d). Confirm the exact figure against the Fair Work Ombudsman's MA000100 pay guide.

### What changed about SCHADS sleepovers on 1 June 2026? 

From the first full pay period on or after 1 June 2026, following FWC decision [2025] FWCFB 292, work before and after a sleepover is treated as a single continuous shift for overtime purposes. A sleepover also no longer counts as a rest break between shifts under clause 25.4.

### What is the minimum payment if a worker is woken during a sleepover? 

One hour at overtime rates per call-out, regardless of how brief the actual work was. If the worker is woken twice, each call-out attracts its own one-hour minimum.

### Is travel time between clients paid under SCHADS? 

Yes. Travel time between clients during a shift is paid at the worker's ordinary rate. Travel from home to the first client and from the last client home is not paid.

### What is the SCHADS vehicle allowance in 2026? 

$1.01 per kilometre from 1 July 2026 under clause 20.3, up from $0.99/km in 2025-26. It applies when a worker uses their own vehicle between clients or to transport a participant.

### What is the difference between a sleepover and an active overnight shift? 

A sleepover is where the employee is permitted to sleep and only required to assist if needed, paid at the flat $62.87 allowance per night. An active overnight shift is where the employee is awake and working throughout, paid at applicable hourly and penalty rates for every hour worked.
