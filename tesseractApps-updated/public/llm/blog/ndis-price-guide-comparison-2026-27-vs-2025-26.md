# NDIS Price Guide 2026-27 vs 2025-26: Line-by-Line Breakdown of Support Item Changes

Side-by-side comparison of NDIS support item codes and price limits for 2026-27 vs 2025-26

The NDIS Pricing Schedule 2026-27 took effect on 1 July 2026, and against the 2025-26 catalogue, 25 support items were removed and 33 new ones added across a catalogue of 624 current codes. Layered on top of that are rate changes that moved in different directions depending on the support type, plus a handful of structural changes that affect how items are claimed, not just what they cost. For a billing or claims team, checking the new rates is not really the task this year. The task is reconciling your price book item by item against a catalogue that changed shape as well as price, then updating service agreements and claim templates to match.

This is that line-by-line comparison.

## Support worker rates: 2025-26 vs 2026-27

Support worker rates rose approximately 4.8% across the board, reflecting the Fair Work Commission's 2026 [SCHADS Award](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-2026-the-complete-guide-for-ndis-and-disability-providers) decision and the increase in the Superannuation Guarantee to 12%.

**Support worker rate comparison: 2025-26 vs 2026-27**

- **Weekday daytime:** $70.23/hr (2025-26) to $73.58/hr (2026-27)
- **Evening:** proportional increase to $81.07/hr (2026-27)
- **Saturday:** proportional increase to $103.54/hr (2026-27)
- **Sunday:** proportional increase to $133.50/hr (2026-27)
- **Public holiday:** proportional increase to $163.46/hr (2026-27)

The 2025-26 comparison figures for evening, Saturday, Sunday, and public holiday rates are not published as a single confirmed line in publicly available sources. Before updating your price book, pull last year's exact figures from your own records for those categories rather than assuming a flat 4.8% applies uniformly, since penalty rate structures do not always move in perfect lockstep with the base rate.

## Therapy rates: some up, some down, most unchanged

This is where checking every line matters most. The 2026-27 review did not apply a blanket increase to allied health. It benchmarked each discipline separately against Medicare and private health insurance data from more than 16 million therapy claims, and the outcomes varied.

**Allied health rate comparison: 2025-26 vs 2026-27**

- **Psychology:** $232.99/hr to $252.99/hr — up $20.00 (+8.6%)
- **Dietetics:** $188.99/hr to $178.99/hr — down $10.00
- **Exercise physiology:** $166.99/hr to $161.99/hr — down $5.00
- **Occupational therapy:** $193.99/hr — unchanged
- **Speech pathology:** $193.99/hr — unchanged
- **Physiotherapy:** $183.99/hr — unchanged
- **Podiatry:** $188.99/hr — unchanged

Audiology was also held at its 2025-26 rate, though we have not been able to independently confirm its exact dollar figure for this comparison. Check it against the published Pricing Schedule directly rather than assuming it moved with the rest of the frozen group. Psychology was the only therapy discipline to receive an increase this review.

**Nursing rates: 2025-26 vs 2026-27**

- **Enrolled nurse:** $99.88/hr to $103.44/hr
- **Registered nurse:** $123.65/hr to $128.05/hr
- **Clinical nurse:** $143.04/hr to $148.13/hr
- **Nurse practitioner:** $176.85/hr to $183.15/hr

All four nursing levels rose by approximately 3.6%, in line with the broader wage-related cost increases flowing through this year's schedule. If your billing system applies a flat percentage adjustment across all therapy line items, that will overpay on dietetics and exercise physiology and underpay on psychology, in both cases producing a claim that does not match the Pricing Schedule.

## Support coordination and plan management: mostly frozen

Support coordination rates were not increased for 2026-27, and providers have raised this as a point of ongoing concern given rates have not moved since 2019-20.

- **Support Coordination Level 1:** $80.06/hr — unchanged
- **Support Coordination Level 2:** $100.14/hr — unchanged
- **Support Coordination Level 3:** $190.54/hr — unchanged
- **Plan management monthly fee (14_034_0127_8_3):** $104.45/month — unchanged

The plan management monthly fee itself is unchanged, but a related line item has been removed entirely. The separate plan management establishment or set-up fee no longer appears in the 2026-27 schedule. If a plan-managed participant is billed a set-up fee this year, that is worth querying against the current catalogue rather than assuming it is still a valid claimable item.

## Structural changes that are not about price at all

Several of this year's biggest practical changes for billing and claims teams do not show up as a rate movement at all. They show up as new item numbers and restructured claim types.

### Therapy items split into six line items per discipline

The old single claim type system for allied health has been replaced. Each therapy discipline now has separate item numbers for direct service, cancellation, non-face-to-face support, provider travel, NDIA-requested reports, and telehealth. Telehealth did not exist as a standalone item in 2025-26; it is new and separately claimable in 2026-27. If your billing templates still reference last year's single item number per discipline, they will need to be rebuilt against the new structure, not just re-priced.

### Short Term Accommodation has been restructured

STA now includes separate line items for participant accommodation and support worker accommodation, rather than one combined item. This affects how STA claims should be itemised going forward.

### SIL has a new registration group and item prefix

[Supported Independent Living](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/ndis-sil-mandatory-registration-2026-checklist) items now carry a 0138 prefix in place of the previous 0115, alongside the roughly 4.8% rate increase applied to SIL supports. For claims spanning the 1 July transition, supports delivered before that date use the old 0115 codes, and supports delivered on or after it use the new 0138 codes.

### Provider travel for therapy stays capped at 50% of the hourly rate

This rule is not new for 2026-27; it carried over from the 2025-26 schedule. It is easy to overlook when reconciling therapy line items against the new six-item structure, so it is worth confirming your billing system still applies it correctly under the new item numbers.

## The bigger structural shift behind this year's changes

Two things are worth understanding beyond the individual line items. First, the NDIA has stopped calling these figures price limits and now frames them as appropriate and reasonable maximum prices, language introduced ahead of a proposed Ministerial power to make binding pricing determinations under the National Disability Insurance Scheme Amendment (Securing the NDIS for Future Generations) Bill 2026, introduced into Parliament in May 2026. Second, the claiming rules and updated Support Catalogue that would normally accompany the Pricing Schedule have not been republished yet. The price tables are confirmed and in effect; the detailed rules for edge cases still sit with last year's guidance until the NDIA releases an update.

Neither of these is a reason to delay updating your price book. Both are reasons to keep checking the NDIA's pricing page for updates rather than treating 1 July as the final word on 2026-27.

There is also a change coming that sits just outside this comparison but is worth flagging now: from 1 January 2027, participants using an unregistered provider for community access supports will see the claimable rate reduced. It does not affect your 2026-27 price book today, but if your service works with self-managed participants and unregistered arrangements, it is worth building into your next planning cycle rather than treating it as a surprise in six months.

## What billing and claims teams should reconcile this week

- **Rebuild therapy item templates** around the new six-line-item structure per discipline, including the new standalone telehealth items, rather than re-pricing the old single item numbers.
- **Check every therapy discipline individually.** Psychology rose, dietetics and exercise physiology fell, occupational therapy, speech pathology, and physiotherapy stayed flat, and podiatry, audiology, and nursing need their exact figures confirmed against the published schedule. A single blanket adjustment will get most of these wrong.
- **Remove the plan management set-up fee** from any active billing templates if it is still configured as claimable.
- **Split SIL claims at 1 July,** using 0115 codes before the effective date and 0138 codes from it, with the roughly 4.8% rate increase applied only to the new codes.
- **Re-itemise Short Term Accommodation claims** into participant accommodation and support worker accommodation components going forward.
- **Confirm evening, Saturday, Sunday, and public holiday support worker rates** against your own 2025-26 records rather than assuming a uniform 4.8% uplift across every time category.
