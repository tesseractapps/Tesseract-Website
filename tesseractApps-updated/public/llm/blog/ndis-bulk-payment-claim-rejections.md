# Why NDIS Bulk Payment Claims Keep Getting Rejected (And How to Stop It Costing You Weekly)

PACE bulk upload rejections are silently eroding provider margins. The seven causes, how to prevent them, and what the fix costs weekly.

## A rejected NDIS claim isn't an error. It's a leak.

Under PACE, almost every payment request goes through the bulk upload process -even single claims. That means every rejected line item is not just an admin annoyance, it's revenue sitting in limbo. Multiply it by weekly claim runs, across dozens of participants, and you've built yourself a slow, quiet outflow that finance won't spot until end of quarter.

If your provider is submitting claims by uploading CSVs into the myplace portal and hoping for the best, this article is for you.

## The seven reasons NDIS bulk claims get rejected

Almost every rejection in the wild traces back to one of these:

1. Service booking mismatch. The line item is being claimed against a category, provider, or dates that don't line up with what's actually on the participant's plan under PACE. This is the number-one cause and the hardest to catch manually.
2. Support-item code errors. The wrong item number for the state, the wrong ratio for group supports, or a code that was quietly retired in the last price guide update.
3. Pricing above the cap. Someone put the raw internal rate into the bulk file instead of the NDIS-approved rate for that item, region, and time of day.
4. Overlapping service times. Two workers claiming for the same participant at the same minute -usually a rostering artefact from a copy-paste shift.
5. Plan expiry or plan review gap. The claim is dated inside a period where the participant's plan wasn't active, or where the new plan hadn't been re-endorsed.
6. Participant status change. The participant left your service, moved to a different plan management arrangement, or their consent has lapsed and nobody updated the record.
7. Bulk file structure errors. Wrong column order, missing mandatory fields, or a stale template pulled from a shared drive that nobody has updated since the last portal change.

## Why 'we'll clean it up on Friday' is a losing strategy

Under PACE, a rejected claim can't just be fixed inline. The rejected line has to be removed, corrected, and re-submitted as a new payment request. If your team is doing that in a spreadsheet, you're carrying three costs:

- The re-work labour -often 15 to 40 minutes per rejection, across support workers, coordinators, and finance.
- The cashflow gap -the claim sits unpaid until it's re-submitted and cleared. On a mid-size provider that can be tens of thousands rolling forward month on month.
- The audit exposure -rejections that never get re-submitted look like unbilled service delivery, which is a red flag the moment an internal audit or Commission review starts pulling records.

## How to stop claim rejections before they happen

### 1. Validate at the point of rostering, not the point of upload

If your platform lets a support worker be rostered for a shift the participant's plan can't fund, the rejection is already baked in. NDIS-native operational platforms check service bookings, support-item codes, and pricing at the moment a shift is scheduled -hours or days before the claim is generated.

### 2. Generate the bulk file from service delivery data, not from a spreadsheet

Every re-keyed value between the roster, the timesheet, and the bulk upload is a rejection waiting to happen. The claim file should be a read-only export of what actually happened in service delivery, generated by the same system that scheduled and delivered the shift.

### 3. Track rejections with a reason code, an owner, and an SLA

A rejection dashboard with reason codes and named owners turns a mystery into a manageable process. Fifteen rejections a week with clear owners and a two-day fix SLA is a normal operation. Fifteen rejections a week piling up in a shared inbox is a crisis in slow motion.

### 4. Reconcile weekly, not monthly

The gap between service delivery and payment should be measured in days, not weeks. Weekly reconciliation catches PACE issues while the shift is still fresh in the worker's memory and the participant's record is still current.

## What a fixed workflow looks like

On a modern NDIS operational platform, the ideal claim run looks like this: a shift is rostered against the participant's service booking; the platform validates that the booking, item code, and rate all agree; the shift is delivered and the worker clocks off; the claim line is generated automatically; the bulk file is submitted; the response file is reconciled inside the platform, with rejections routed to an owner and a re-submission workflow.

If that's not what your Fridays look like, your platform is costing you more than its licence fee.
