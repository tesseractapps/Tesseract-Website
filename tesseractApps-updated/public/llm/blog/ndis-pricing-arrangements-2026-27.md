# NDIS Pricing Arrangements 2026-27: What's Changed and What It Means for Your Provider?

A breakdown of the NDIS Pricing Schedule 2026-27: check what Finance and Service Managers must update before the next billing cycle.

The National Disability Insurance Agency released the results of its 2026-27 Annual Pricing Review on 22 June 2026, with these price changes taking effect from 1 July 2026. If your service agreements, billing system, or payroll still reference last financial year's figures, every invoice you raise from that date carries a small but real risk of over-claiming or under-claiming.

This year's update is bigger than a straightforward rate adjustment. Alongside the annual price changes, the NDIA restructured how it publishes pricing information, changed how Supported Independent Living is registered and coded, and began phasing down a loading some providers have relied on for years. Here is what actually changed, what is still only proposed, and what Finance and Service Managers need to update before the next billing cycle.

## The 2026-27 NDIS Pricing Arrangements at a glance

- **Effective date:** 1 July 2026 (released 22 June 2026)
- **Support worker weekday daytime rate:** $70.23 to $73.58 per hour
- **Plan management fee:** unchanged at $104.45 per month
- **SIL support items:** recoded from 0115 to 0138 from 1 July 2026
- **Publishing format:** one combined PAPL document replaced by three separate publications
- **TTP loading:** being phased down, subject to benchmarking requirements

## The publishing structure has changed too

In previous years, everything sat in one combined document, the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits, commonly shortened to PAPL and still often called the NDIS price guide out of habit even though that is no longer the NDIA's official name for it. For 2026-27, that single document has been split into three separate publications:

- **The Annual Pricing Review,** which sets out the evidence and reasoning behind the year's pricing decisions.
- **The NDIS Pricing Schedule,** which lists support item numbers, names, units, and the maximum national, remote, and very remote prices effective from 1 July 2026.
- **The NDIS Support Catalogue,** which sits alongside the Pricing Schedule as the reference for support item structure.

Worth flagging for your compliance team: the NDIA has published the price tables, but not yet a full rules document covering claiming, travel, cancellation, and non-face-to-face provisions in the same detail the old PAPL provided. If your billing system relies on those rules for edge cases, check current guidance with your software provider or the NDIA rather than assuming last year's rules still apply in full.

## What actually changed in the rates?

Rate increases largely flow from two sources: the Fair Work Commission's 2026 SCHADS Award decision and the increase in the Superannuation Guarantee, both of which push up the cost of delivering support.

### Support worker rates rose

The standard weekday daytime rate for core supports moved from $70.23 to $73.58 per hour, roughly a 4.8% increase, with proportional increases carried through evening, night, weekend, and public holiday rates. If your price book or invoicing templates still show last year's figures, this is the first thing to update.

### Therapy rates moved in different directions

The NDIA compared NDIS prices against Medicare and private health insurance data across more than 16 million therapy claims, and the results were not uniform:

- **Psychology rates rose** from $232.99 to $252.99 per hour, reflecting that NDIS rates were sitting below what Medicare and private health funds pay. This change is intended to make it easier for participants to access psychologists.
- **Dietetics rates fell** from $188.99 to $178.99 per hour, because NDIS rates were assessed as higher than the Medicare and private health fund comparison.
- **Exercise physiology rates were also adjusted down,** having been assessed as above the Medicare comparison benchmark.

If your service delivers a mix of these supports, do not assume every line item moved in the same direction. Check each support item individually against the new Pricing Schedule rather than applying a flat percentage increase across your whole price book.

### Some fees stayed flat

The monthly plan management fee is confirmed unchanged at $104.45 (item 14_034_0127_8_3). The NDIA has flagged a review of whether a flat monthly fee still makes sense as the sector changes, so treat this as stable for now rather than permanently fixed.

## Item code and registration changes: the NDIS support item updates to check first

The most operationally significant structural change is in Supported Independent Living. A new registration group has been introduced for SIL, and the associated support items now carry a 0138 prefix in place of the previous 0115.

This is not just a renumbering exercise for your records. For SIL supports delivered before 1 July 2026, continue claiming under the existing 0115 codes. For supports delivered on or after that date, use the new 0138 item numbers. Get this wrong and claims can be rejected or, worse, processed incorrectly against the wrong registration group. If SIL is a meaningful part of your service mix, this should be the first thing your billing team checks against the new Support Catalogue.

## A remote loading change still in the pipeline

Not everything in the 2026-27 Annual Pricing Review is locked in yet. The NDIA has proposed two new isolated town classifications, with Type 1 Isolated Towns treated as MM6 for remote price loadings and travel arrangements, but this is currently a recommendation, not a confirmed price, and sits outside the Pricing Schedule that took effect on 1 July 2026. If your service operates in or near a regional area, track this specifically rather than assuming your current remoteness classification carries forward unchanged. Only figures in the published Pricing Schedule are safe to build into your billing system today.

## The TTP loading is being phased down

Providers claiming the Temporary Transformation Payment loading need to check their continued eligibility. The loading is being phased down under the 2026-27 arrangements, and providers must still meet the underlying benchmarking requirements to claim it. Claiming TTP without meeting those requirements is treated as a compliance breach, not just an overpayment to be corrected later, so this is worth a direct conversation with your finance team rather than an assumption that last year's eligibility still applies.

## The mid-year claiming trap

Here is where a lot of providers get caught out every year, and 2026-27 is no exception. A single fortnightly pay period can straddle 30 June and 1 July. Supports delivered before the effective date must be claimed at the old price limits, and supports delivered on or after it use the new limits, regardless of when the pay run or invoice is actually processed.

If your rostering or billing system does not stamp every shift and invoice line with an accurate service date, this is genuinely easy to get wrong at scale, particularly across a large casual workforce delivering shifts every day of the week through the transition period. The fix is not a manual reminder to staff. It is a system that applies the correct price limit automatically based on the actual date the support was delivered, not the date it happens to be processed.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did the NDIS Pricing Arrangements 2026-27 take effect?

From 1 July 2026, following the Annual Pricing Review results released by the NDIA on 22 June 2026.

### Is the NDIS Price Guide the same thing as the NDIS Pricing Schedule?

Functionally, yes. The NDIA has moved away from the combined PAPL document many providers still call the price guide, replacing it with the NDIS Pricing Schedule as the source for maximum prices, alongside separate Annual Pricing Review and Support Catalogue publications.

### Do I need to update my service agreements immediately?

Yes, and you need to speak with each participant before applying new rates, regardless of whether they are agency managed, plan managed, or self managed. Update billing systems and service agreements together rather than treating them as separate tasks.

### What happens to invoices for shifts that straddle 30 June and 1 July?

Supports delivered before 1 July are claimed at the old price limits, and supports delivered on or after that date use the new limits, based on the actual service date, not the date the invoice is processed.

## What to update before your next billing cycle

- **Service agreements.** Under the new arrangements, providers must speak with participants before changing service agreement rates, whether the participant is agency managed, plan managed, or self managed. Build this conversation into your update timeline, not just your billing system update.
- **Billing and invoicing systems.** Update price books against the new Pricing Schedule item by item, not with a blanket percentage adjustment, given rates moved in different directions across support types.
- **Payroll.** Confirm support worker pay rates and on-costs align with the SCHADS changes underpinning the new price limits, so your margin assumptions hold up once the new prices are in effect.
- **SIL claims specifically.** Confirm your team is using 0115 codes for supports delivered before 1 July 2026 and 0138 codes from that date, with no gap or overlap in your claiming records.
- **TTP eligibility.** Reconfirm benchmarking requirements are still met before continuing to claim the loading.
- **Historical claims audit.** Spot-check invoices raised either side of 1 July for pay periods that straddled the transition, to catch mid-year claiming errors early rather than at your next NDIA compliance check.
