# Calculating Overtime, Public Holiday and Weekend Penalties Under SCHADS

Step-by-step worked examples of SCHADS overtime, weekend and public holiday pay, including QLD, NSW, VIC, WA holiday variations.

Penalty rates are where the SCHADS Award gets expensive fast. What payroll owes on any given shift depends on the day, the time, the stream and whether hours pushed past ordinary limits. Get one variable wrong and the error repeats on every similar shift until someone does the audit.

This guide sets out every penalty rate, overtime rate and public holiday rate under the SCHADS Award with step-by-step worked examples and a state-by-state public holiday table. All rates reflect the 4.75% increase effective from the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026.

## Weekend penalty rates under SCHADS

Weekend rates under the award are expressed as multiples of the ordinary base rate. They apply to all ordinary hours worked on Saturday and Sunday and differ between permanent and casual employees because the 25% casual loading stacks on top of the penalty for weekends and public holidays.

| Day | Permanent (FT/PT) | Casual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saturday | 150% | 175% |
| Sunday | 200% | 225% |
| Public holiday | 250% | 275% |


For a permanent Level 2 SACS employee at an indicative 2026 base of $36.22/hr:

- Saturday rate: $54.33/hr
- Sunday rate: $72.44/hr
- Public holiday rate: $90.55/hr

For a casual at the same level (indicative base $45.28/hr including loading):

- Saturday: $63.39/hr (175% of $36.22)
- Sunday: $81.50/hr (225% of $36.22)
- Public holiday: $99.61/hr (275% of $36.22)

The casual calculation applies the loading to the base rate, then multiplies by the penalty percentage. It does not simply add 25% to the permanent penalty rate.

## Shift loadings: afternoons and nights

Shift loadings apply to the whole shift, not just the hours that fall outside ordinary time. If a shift finishes after 8pm on a Monday to Friday, the entire shift attracts the afternoon loading. If it finishes after midnight or starts before 6am Monday to Friday, it attracts the night loading.

| Shift type | Loading |
| --- | --- |
| Afternoon (finishes after 8pm Mon-Fri) | 112.5% of base rate |
| Night (finishes after midnight or starts before 6am Mon-Fri) | 115% of base rate |


Shift loadings and weekend penalties do not stack. Only the higher rate applies. A shift that finishes after 8pm on a Friday attracts the afternoon loading or the Friday ordinary rate, whichever is higher. The same shift on a Saturday attracts the Saturday penalty rate, not the Saturday rate plus an afternoon loading.

## Overtime rates by stream

SCHADS overtime rates are where the stream distinction matters most. The point at which the higher rate kicks in differs between streams.

**SACS and Crisis Accommodation employees (Schedules B and C):**

- Monday to Saturday overtime: 150% for the first three hours, 200% after that
- Sunday overtime: 200% for all hours
- Public holiday overtime: 250% for all hours

**Home Care, Disability Services and Family Day Care employees (Schedules D, E and F):**

- Monday to Saturday overtime: 150% for the first two hours, 200% after that
- Sunday overtime: 200% for all hours
- Public holiday overtime: 250% for all hours

The difference between a two-hour and three-hour first band matters on long shifts. A SACS worker doing four hours of overtime on a Saturday has 150% applying for the first three hours and 200% for the fourth. A Home Care employee doing the same four hours tips into 200% after just two.

Overtime rates replace weekend and penalty rates. They do not stack on top of them. A Sunday overtime hour is paid at 200%, not at 200% Sunday penalty plus overtime uplift. The higher of the two applicable rates applies.

Overtime is triggered when an employee works beyond their rostered hours, exceeds 38 hours in a week (averaged), or works outside the ordinary span of hours (broadly 6am to 8pm Monday to Sunday for most SCHADS roles). Casuals attract overtime after more than 10 hours in a day or 38 hours in a week, always applying the first band rate.

## A worked example: permanent SACS Level 2 working a long Saturday

A permanent Level 2 SACS employee at $36.22/hr base is rostered 8am to 5pm on a Saturday, but stays until 7pm covering a staff shortage.

- 8am to 5pm (9 hours ordinary Saturday work): 150% x $36.22 = $54.33/hr x 9 = $489.00
- 5pm to 7pm (2 hours Saturday overtime, within the 3-hour first band): 150% x $36.22 = $54.33/hr x 2 = $108.66

Total before super: $597.66 for the 11-hour Saturday. If the same worker had stayed until 8:30pm, the final 1.5 hours beyond the 3-hour overtime threshold would move to 200% ($72.44/hr).

## A worked example: casual Home Care Level 2 on a public holiday

A casual Home Care Disability Level 2 employee has an indicative 2026 base of approximately $28.86/hr. On a public holiday they earn 275% of the base rate: $28.86 x 2.75 = $79.37/hr. A three-hour visit pays approximately $238.10 before super and allowances, and with the two-hour minimum engagement, a 90-minute visit still costs $158.74. That is the figure the NDIS public holiday price limit has to cover.

## State-by-state public holiday variations: why the date matters

The SCHADS Award sets a flat 250% (permanent) or 275% (casual) rate for any work performed on a public holiday. What varies by state is which days qualify as public holidays in the first place.

NDIS providers operating across multiple states may have workers entitled to public holiday pay on different dates for the same service type. A worker in Queensland working on Queensland Labour Day earns 250% for that day. A worker in Victoria doing the same work on the same date earns their ordinary rate because Victoria's Labour Day falls in March, not May.

National public holidays in 2026 apply across all states: New Year's Day, Australia Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, ANZAC Day, Christmas Day and Boxing Day substitute (Mon 28 Dec in most states). One scheduling note: ANZAC Day falls on Saturday 25 April. Only ACT, NSW and WA observe a substitute Monday. In Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and Tasmania the public holiday pay applies on the Saturday itself, and Monday 27 April is an ordinary weekday.

## How overtime interacts with public holidays?

Work on a public holiday that runs into overtime is paid at 250% for all hours. The public holiday rate replaces other penalty and overtime rates for those hours. A worker recalled after finishing their rostered hours on a public holiday is paid at 250% for each hour worked, subject to the applicable minimum engagement period.

## Common payroll errors with penalties and overtime

**Applying the wrong overtime band for the stream.** SACS employees get three hours at 150% before moving to 200%. Home Care and Disability Services employees move after two. Applying the two-hour rule uniformly across all streams understates the 150% band for every SACS and crisis worker.

**Stacking loadings that should not stack.** Afternoon loading and Saturday penalty do not apply simultaneously. Only the Saturday penalty applies on a Saturday evening. An evening loading on top of that is an overpayment that complicates reconciliation when rates change.

**Missing state-specific public holidays for multi-state providers.** A roster built around national public holidays only misses state-specific dates entirely, creating public holiday underpayments for every affected worker on those dates.

**Using the casual rate as the base for penalty calculations.** The penalty percentage applies to the base rate, not the casual-loaded rate. Saturday casual at 175% means $36.22 x 1.75 = $63.39, not $45.28 x 1.50.

## SCHADS overtime and penalty rates FAQ

#### **What is the Saturday penalty rate under SCHADS?** 

150% of the base rate for permanent full-time and part-time employees, 175% for casuals. At an indicative 2026 Level 2 SACS base of $36.22/hr, the Saturday permanent rate is approximately $54.33/hr and the casual rate approximately $63.39/hr.

#### **What is the Sunday rate under SCHADS?**

200% for permanent employees, 225% for casuals. At Level 2 SACS these work out to approximately $72.44/hr permanent and $81.50/hr casual.

#### **What is the public holiday rate under SCHADS?** 

250% for permanent employees, 275% for casuals. At Level 2 SACS these work out to approximately $90.55/hr permanent and $99.61/hr casual.

#### **Does overtime stack on top of weekend penalties under SCHADS?** 

No. Overtime rates replace weekend and penalty rates. A Sunday overtime hour is 200%, not 200% plus an overtime uplift.

#### **How does SCHADS overtime differ by stream?** 

SACS and Crisis Accommodation employees: 150% for the first three overtime hours, 200% after. Home Care, Disability Services and Family Day Care: 150% for the first two overtime hours, 200% after. Sunday and public holiday overtime is 200% and 250% respectively for all streams.

#### **Do state public holidays affect SCHADS penalty rates?** 

Yes. The 250% (permanent) or 275% (casual) public holiday rate applies on any day that is a public holiday in the relevant state. State-specific holidays such as QLD Labour Day, VIC Melbourne Cup Day and WA Day affect which days attract public holiday rates and vary by state.
