# Why Manual Rostering Is Costing You More Than You Think

Manual rostering may seem manageable, but the true costs are lost productivity, compliance risks, higher labour expenses, and staff turnover.

> [!INFO]
> Key Takeaways: Manual rostering costs NDIS providers 10-15 hours/week in admin time. Compliance risks include missed certifications, award breaches, and fatigue violations. Automated rostering reduces costs, improves staff retention, and enhances care quality.


The care sector is under increasing pressure to do more with less. For NDIS providers juggling [complex rosters](/capabilities/rostering-scheduling), workforce compliance, and tight margins, manual rostering is often the default approach. But what seems manageable on the surface can quietly drain your organisation's resources, resilience, and staff morale.

## The Hidden Costs of Manual Rostering

### Lost Productivity and Administrative Burden

Roster managers in NDIS organisations spend an estimated 10-15 hours per week on [manual scheduling tasks](/capabilities/rostering-scheduling) — matching availability, managing last-minute changes, and handling shift swaps. That's the equivalent of nearly two full working days consumed by administration rather than strategic planning or participant outcomes.

### Compliance Risks

Manual rostering significantly increases the risk of [non-compliance](/capabilities/compliance-audit-readiness). Without automated checks, organisations can inadvertently schedule staff with expired certifications, breach SCHADS Award conditions, violate fatigue management requirements, or miss mandatory break requirements.

In a highly regulated environment like the NDIS, these oversights can result in financial penalties, [audit failures](/capabilities/compliance-audit-readiness), and reputational damage.

### Higher Labour Costs

Without data-driven insights, [manual rostering](/capabilities/rostering-scheduling) often results in overstaffing during low-demand periods and understaffing during peak times. Skill mismatches — placing overqualified staff in entry-level roles — further inflate labour costs without improving care outcomes.

### Reduced Staff Engagement and Retention

With **78% of NDIS providers** reporting difficulties retaining support workers, roster management practices play a significant role. Unpredictable schedules, last-minute changes, and perceived unfairness in shift allocation are major contributors to dissatisfaction and turnover.

### Limited Data and Insights

Manual systems provide little visibility into rostering patterns, staff utilisation, or cost trends. Without this data, identifying inefficiencies or making evidence-based decisions becomes nearly impossible.

## Why It Matters

The shift from manual to automated rostering isn't just about saving time — it's about building a more resilient, compliant, and engaged workforce. Providers who invest in smarter rostering tools consistently report improved staff satisfaction, reduced compliance incidents, and lower administrative costs.
