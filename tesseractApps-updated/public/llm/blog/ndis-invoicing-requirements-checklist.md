# NDIS Invoicing Requirements Checklist: What Every Provider Needs to Know

Get NDIS invoicing wrong and your claims get rejected, your cash flow suffers, and your audit risk goes up.

If you have run a business outside the NDIS sector, you know how invoicing works. You deliver a service, you send an invoice, you get paid. The invoice needs a date, a description, an amount, and your ABN.

NDIS invoicing is different. Every claim is tied to a specific support item in the [NDIS Support Catalogue](/ndis-glossary). Every support item has a line item code, a unit of measure, a price limit, and a set of delivery conditions. Your invoice is not just billing for a service. It is a claim against a participant's approved plan funding, and it has to be structured correctly or it will be rejected.

For providers who are new to the scheme, or who are scaling quickly and relying on manual processes, the invoicing requirements can become a significant source of operational drag and cash flow delay. This article breaks down exactly what NDIS invoicing requires, what auditors look for, and what happens when you get it wrong.

## The NDIS Invoicing Checklist: What Every Invoice Must Include

Regardless of whether you are billing a self-managed participant, a [plan-managed participant](/solutions/support-coordination), or claiming directly through the NDIS portal for agency-managed participants, your invoice needs to contain the following:

### For all NDIS invoices:

1. Your organisation's name and ABN
2. The participant's full name and NDIS number
3. The date the support was delivered, not the invoice date
4. A description of the support matching the relevant NDIS support item
5. The NDIS support item code from the current [Support Catalogue](/ndis-glossary)
6. The unit of measure (hourly, daily, each)
7. The quantity of units delivered
8. The price per unit, which must not exceed the NDIS price limit for that item
9. The total amount claimed
10. Your payment details

### For plan-managed participants, additionally:

- The plan manager's name and contact details
- Reference to the relevant service agreement

Missing any of these elements can result in the invoice being rejected by the plan manager or the NDIA portal, which means delayed payment and additional administrative work to resubmit.

## The Most Common NDIS Invoicing Mistakes

### Using the wrong support item code

The NDIS Support Catalogue is updated regularly. Price limits change. Item codes are occasionally modified or retired. If you are working from an outdated catalogue, or if your team is selecting support items from memory, errors are inevitable. A claim lodged under the wrong support item will either be rejected or, worse, paid and then flagged in an audit.

### Claiming above the price limit

Every support item has a maximum price. Claiming above it, even by a small margin, results in rejection. If your prices are set manually and have not been updated since the last catalogue revision, you may be billing at rates that are no longer valid.

### Mismatched service dates

The date on your invoice should reflect when the support was delivered, not when the invoice was generated. A claim where the service date does not match the shift record raises an immediate red flag with plan managers and NDIA reviewers.

### Bulk invoicing without itemisation

Some providers send a single invoice covering multiple supports across a billing period without breaking down each support delivery individually. Many plan managers require itemised invoices, one line per support delivered.

### Not retaining the underlying shift records

The invoice is the claim. The shift record, the [timesheet](/capabilities/timesheets-payroll-alignment), the [case note](/capabilities/voice-notes), and the signed service delivery record are the evidence. If you cannot produce the underlying records when asked, the claim is unverifiable. In an audit, unverifiable claims are treated as unsubstantiated.

> A rejected invoice is not just a billing error. It is a cash flow problem. For smaller providers, delayed payment from a handful of rejected claims can create real financial pressure within a single pay cycle.

## What Auditors Actually Look for in Invoicing Records

When the NDIS Commission examines your invoicing records, they are not just checking that invoices exist. They are looking for consistency across three layers.

### Layer 1: The invoice itself

Does it contain all required fields? Are the support item codes correct? Does the price match the current catalogue? Is the participant's NDIS number accurate?

### Layer 2: The shift record

Does the [timesheet](/capabilities/timesheets-payroll-alignment) confirm that the support was delivered on the date claimed, for the duration claimed, by a worker rostered to that participant?

### Layer 3: The case note

Does the [case note](/capabilities/voice-notes) for that shift describe supports consistent with what was invoiced? If the invoice claims two hours of personal care but the case note describes a community outing, that is a discrepancy that will be investigated.

All three layers need to be consistent with each other. Providers who have strong invoicing but poor documentation, or strong case notes but no timesheet records, are still exposed.

> [!WARNING]
> Common audit triggers: Price limit exceedances even by small amounts. Support item codes that do not match the service agreement. Service dates absent from timesheet or attendance records. Gaps between invoice dates and service delivery of more than 90 days. Participant billing that continues after a service agreement has expired. Invoices for supports not listed in the participant's active plan.


## Self-Managed, Plan-Managed, and Agency-Managed: What Changes

**Self-managed participants** pay you directly from their own funds. The compliance burden still applies but the claim does not pass through a plan manager or the NDIA portal.

**Plan-managed participants** have a registered plan manager who processes claims on their behalf. You invoice the plan manager, not the participant. Plan managers often have their own formatting requirements or submission portals in addition to the NDIS requirements.

**Agency-managed participants** are funded directly through the NDIA. You claim through the NDIS myplace provider portal. The portal validates your claim against the participant's plan in real time. If the support item, price, or dates do not match what is in the plan, the claim is rejected at submission.

## Auto-Invoicing: What It Is and Why It Matters

Manual invoicing, generating [invoices from timesheets by hand](/capabilities/ndis-claiming-invoicing), entering support codes one by one, checking price limits against the current catalogue, is viable at small scale. At any meaningful volume, it is a bottleneck and a risk.

Auto-invoicing means your platform generates invoices automatically from confirmed, [approved shift records](/capabilities/timesheets-payroll-alignment). When a shift is delivered and approved, the invoice is created with the correct support item code pulled from the service agreement, the price applied from the current catalogue, the participant details populated from their profile, and the service date matched to the actual delivery.

This eliminates the category of error that comes from manual data entry. It also accelerates your billing cycle. Instead of processing invoices at the end of the month, they can be generated and submitted within days of service delivery, improving cash flow without any additional administrative effort.

## What Good Looks Like in Practice

The right invoicing workflow looks like this:

1. A service agreement is set up with the correct NDIS support item codes and price limits
2. A shift is delivered, clocked, and approved through your [timesheet workflow](/capabilities/timesheets-payroll-alignment)
3. The invoice is generated automatically from that approved shift, no re-entry
4. The invoice contains all required fields, pulled from participant and service agreement records
5. The invoice is submitted to the plan manager or NDIA portal
6. The invoice links back to the [underlying shift](/capabilities/rostering-scheduling) and [case note](/capabilities/voice-notes) for audit evidence
7. Payments reconcile automatically in [Xero](/capabilities/xero-integration) against outstanding invoices
