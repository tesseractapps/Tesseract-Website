# Single Touch Payroll Phase 2 for SCHADS Employers: Your EOFY Survival Guide

How SCHADS-covered AU disability employers should finalise STP Phase 2, categorise allowances correctly, and handle termination payments at EOFY.

Single Touch Payroll Phase 2 is now the permanent reporting standard for Australian employers, with no further extensions or transition deferrals. For most industries, that's a settled fact of payroll life. For [SCHADS-covered disability employers](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-2026-the-complete-guide-for-ndis-and-disability-providers), STP Phase 2 is a genuinely harder standard to meet, because the award's allowance structure, casual-heavy workforce and high staff turnover all interact directly with the parts of the reporting standard that trip employers up most: allowance categorisation and termination reporting.

This guide walks Payroll Managers through STP Phase 2 for SCHADS employers specifically, why the two categories above cause more errors here than in other sectors, and why getting them wrong at EOFY now carries a bigger downstream cost than it used to.

## What disaggregation of gross actually requires?

STP Phase 2 replaced the old single "gross" figure with a requirement to separately report each component of an employee's pay: paid leave, allowances, [overtime](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-overtime-penalty-rates), bonuses and commissions, director's fees, salary sacrifice, and lump sum payments. The ATO calls this the disaggregation of gross, and the purpose isn't just tidier reporting. This richer data feeds employee income statements, pre-fills tax returns, and flows through to Services Australia in real time for employees with a Centrelink customer reference number, so an incorrectly classified payment doesn't just sit quietly in your payroll system. It can misstate an employee's assessable income for Family Tax Benefit or child support purposes, and it's exactly the kind of error that can hold up your STP finalisation with the ATO.

For a SCHADS employer, this matters more than for most industries because the award itself generates a long list of allowance types across a single roster: travel, transport and fares, sleepover, laundry and uniform, first aid, leading hand or supervisory duties, broken shift, on-call and recall, and higher duties or qualification-based allowances. Each one needs to be mapped to the correct ATO allowance category, not bundled together. A generic retail or hospitality payroll setup rarely has to solve this many allowance types for a single employee, which is exactly why STP Phase 2 catches out disability employers more than most.

## Where SCHADS allowances go wrong in STP Phase 2?

The ATO has specifically flagged incorrect use of the "Other allowances" category as one of the most common STP Phase 2 mistakes. It's also one of the easiest mistakes for a SCHADS employer to make, because several award allowances don't map neatly onto a single obvious ATO category.

A few practical points worth checking against your own configuration:

- Travel and transport allowances generally fall under specific ATO categories such as award transport payments or cents-per-kilometre allowances, not a generic catch-all. Confirm which applies based on how the allowance is actually calculated and paid, since the two are treated differently for withholding purposes.
- Qualification and higher duties allowances typically have their own reporting category rather than sitting in "Other allowances." Getting this wrong doesn't just misreport the payment, it can affect how the ATO's pre-fill logic treats the employee's deduction eligibility.
- All-purpose allowances that form part of overtime are an exception to standard allowance reporting. If an allowance is folded into a rate that then attracts overtime, the ATO requires it to be reported as overtime, not as a separate allowance, because overtime is treated differently for superannuation guarantee purposes.
- Cashed-out leave and unused leave on termination are also exceptions. Amounts paid as cashing out leave in service, or as unused leave on termination, are reported under the relevant leave type, not as an allowance, even where an allowance rate was used to calculate the payment.

Given how many SCHADS employees move between ordinary hours, overtime, [sleepover ](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-sleepover-active-hours-travel)shifts and [broken shifts](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-broken-shifts-rostering-without-breaches) within a single pay cycle, a payroll configuration built once and left unreviewed is the most common source of these errors, not a one-off data entry mistake.

## Termination payments: where high turnover meets high complexity?

Disability and community services has one of the [highest staff turnover](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/hidden-costs-workforce-management) rates of any SCHADS-covered sector, and STP Phase 2 introduced several new payment classifications specifically for termination that many payroll configurations still don't handle correctly.

- Leave, unused leave on termination is its own classification, separate from standard leave categories, introduced specifically for Phase 2 reporting.
- Lump Sum A splits into two types depending on the reason for termination and when the leave accrued: Type R for genuine redundancy, invalidity or early retirement, and Type T for normal termination. Reporting the wrong type doesn't just misreport the payment; it can misstate how much of it is taxed concessionally.
- Lump Sum D covers the tax-free component of a genuine redundancy or approved early retirement payment and must be reported even where it's the only amount paid to that employee in the financial year, a requirement that didn't exist under STP Phase 1.
- ETP codes distinguish life benefit from death benefit payments and the reason for payment. Getting the wrong code assigned affects how the ATO calculates the employee's tax treatment on that payment.
- Cessation reason codes must be reported for every termination, using the ATO's standard set of codes. For a workforce with a high proportion of fixed-term, casual and contract-ending roles, as most SCHADS employers do, this is worth a specific check rather than a default code applied to every exit.

At EOFY specifically, this is where finalisation most often stalls. If a termination processed earlier in the year used the wrong lump sum type or cessation reason, that error carries through to the employee's income statement and can hold up your STP finalisation declaration while it's corrected.

## Why this now affects more than your STP finalisation?

From 1 July 2026, Payday Super requires the Superannuation Guarantee to be paid on the same timeline as wages, and enforcement of that obligation depends directly on the disaggregated data reported through STP Phase 2. If your payroll system incorrectly includes unused leave on termination in the super guarantee calculation base, for example, it will over-report the super liability for that pay event. That's not a shortfall the ATO will chase you for, but it creates a reconciliation discrepancy that can trigger a data-matching enquiry, precisely the kind of thing an EOFY reconciliation should catch before it becomes an external question.

In other words, allowance and termination misclassifications that used to be a tidiness issue inside your own payroll system are now data quality issues the ATO cross-checks against your super payments in near real time.

## Your EOFY checklist for STP Phase 2 as a SCHADS employer

- Audit your [allowance mapping](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-audit-guide-self-assess-fair-work) against the full list of SCHADS allowance types your organisation actually pays, not just the most common ones, and confirm none are defaulting to "Other allowances."
- Check overtime-linked all-purpose allowances are reported as overtime, not as a separate allowance category, for every role where this applies.
- Review every termination processed this financial year for correct lump sum type, ETP code and cessation reason before lodging your STP finalisation declaration.
- Confirm unused leave on termination is excluded from your super guarantee calculation base where it should be, to avoid over-reporting liability that creates a reconciliation gap.
- Treat this as an ongoing configuration review, not an annual one. New allowance types, new award clauses and new termination scenarios appear throughout the year, and Payday Super means data quality issues now surface every pay cycle, not just at EOFY.

## Frequently asked questions

Is STP Phase 2 still being phased in, or is it now compulsory? It's fully compulsory. There are no further extensions or transition deferrals, and every pay run must include disaggregated payment information reported directly to the ATO.

What's the most common STP Phase 2 mistake for disability employers specifically? Defaulting SCHADS allowances, particularly travel, qualification and higher duties allowances, into the generic "Other allowances" category instead of their specific ATO classification. The ATO has flagged this as one of the most common reporting errors across all industries, and disability employers are more exposed to it than most simply because the award generates more allowance types per employee.

Does payroll EOFY 2026 change anything about how terminations are reported? The termination reporting rules themselves, lump sum types, ETP codes, cessation reasons, haven't changed for this EOFY. What has changed is the consequence of getting them wrong: from 1 July 2026, Payday Super enforcement relies on the same disaggregated data, so a termination misclassification can now surface as a superannuation reconciliation issue, not just a tax reporting one.

Do we need to fix historical STP Phase 2 misclassifications, or just get this year right? Fixing this year's finalisation is the immediate priority, but a misclassification that's been repeating pay cycle after pay cycle is worth correcting at the configuration level, not just patching the current year's data. Otherwise the same error reappears at your next ATO STP finalisation.
