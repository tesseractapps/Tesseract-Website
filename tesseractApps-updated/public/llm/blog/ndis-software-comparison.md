# NDIS Software Comparison 2026: 12 Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Platform

Comparing NDIS software? Use these 12 questions to evaluate rostering, claiming, compliance, security, migration, reporting and total software cost.

Choosing NDIS software can be difficult because, at first glance, many platforms appear to offer the same things.

Participant management. Rostering. Case notes. Compliance. Invoicing. Reporting. Mobile access.

But two systems with similar feature lists can work very differently once your team starts using them every day.

That is why an NDIS software comparison should go beyond asking:

**“Does this platform have this feature?”**

A better question is:

**“How does this platform handle the way our organisation actually works?”**

Before choosing your next NDIS software platform, here are 12 questions worth asking.

## 1. Can the software follow our complete provider workflow?

Start with a real workflow from your organisation.

For example:

**Participant intake → service agreement → funding → roster → shift → progress note → timesheet → invoice → claim → reconciliation → reporting**

Ask the software provider to demonstrate that entire process.

This will tell you much more than looking at a dashboard or a list of features.

Pay attention to where information needs to be entered again.

If participant, service, workforce and finance information needs to be repeatedly copied between different parts of the system, your team may still end up doing a large amount of manual administration.

The goal should be to make information move with the workflow.

## 2. How does participant information connect to service delivery?

Participant management should be more than a digital contact list.

Depending on your organisation, you may need access to information such as:

- participant details
- support needs
- goals
- risks
- service agreements
- funding information
- documents
- contacts
- case notes
- progress notes
- incidents
- delivered services

When comparing NDIS software, check whether this information remains connected.

For example, can staff find the information relevant to a participant without searching across multiple systems or folders?

Can management understand what services were delivered and what documentation was created?

As provider operations become more complex, this connection becomes increasingly important.

## 3. What happens when the roster changes?

A rostering demonstration often looks easy when everything goes according to plan.

Real operations are different.

Ask the software provider to show what happens when:

- a support worker becomes unavailable
- a participant cancels
- a replacement worker is required
- a shift changes at short notice
- qualifications or credentials need to be checked
- recurring shifts need to be updated
- a worker has overlapping shifts
- a timesheet does not match the roster

The important question is not simply whether the software can create a shift.

It is how well the system supports the team when something changes.

## 4. What happens after a support is delivered?

This is one of the most important parts of an NDIS software comparison.

A platform may have excellent rostering, but what happens after the shift?

Ask how information moves from:

**Shift completed**

to

**progress note**

to

**timesheet**

to

**payroll**

to

**invoice**

to

**claim**

to

**reconciliation**

If each step requires a separate manual process, the organisation may still have significant administrative work.

A connected workflow can also make it easier to understand where something went wrong.

For example, if an invoice does not look correct, can the team trace it back to the service that was actually delivered?

## 5. How does the platform manage documentation?

NDIS providers can generate a significant amount of documentation.

This may include:

- case notes
- progress notes
- participant documents
- service agreements
- worker records
- incidents
- complaints
- training records
- approvals
- internal actions

When comparing platforms, ask how easy it is to:

- create records
- locate records
- update information
- control access
- understand when something changed
- retrieve information later

Good documentation systems should help teams find information when it is needed, not simply create more places to store files.

## 6. How does incident management work from beginning to end?

Ask for a full incident demonstration.

For example:

**Incident recorded → manager notified → action assigned → investigation → follow-up → review → closure**

Then ask:

- Can responsibilities be assigned?
- Can management see outstanding actions?
- Can supporting information be attached?
- Is the history retained?
- Can the organisation understand who completed each action?
- Can the information be retrieved later?

Incident management should be evaluated as a workflow rather than a form.

## 7. What does “compliance ready” actually mean?

Software does not make an NDIS provider compliant by itself.

Compliance depends on the organisation's people, policies, procedures, training, governance and day-to-day practices.

Technology can, however, make it easier to maintain structured information and evidence.

Instead of asking:

**“Is your platform NDIS compliant?”**

ask more specific questions:

- Can we see who completed an action?
- Are changes recorded?
- Can approval history be retrieved?
- Can expired or missing worker information be identified?
- Can different users have different access?
- Can records be found when required?
- Can relevant evidence be exported?

Specific questions are much more useful than general compliance claims.

## 8. How does invoicing and claiming work?

Finance teams should be involved in the software comparison process.

Ask the vendor to demonstrate:

**Delivered service → invoice → support item → claim → payment result → reconciliation**

Questions to consider include:

- How are invoices generated?
- Where does pricing information come from?
- How are support items managed?
- Can claims be reviewed before submission?
- How are rejected or unsuccessful claims handled?
- How is payment reconciliation managed?
- Can information connect with your accounting workflow?

This is another area where the complete process matters more than a single feature.

## 9. How does the software support workforce management?

Rostering is only one part of workforce management.

Depending on the size of your organisation, you may also need to consider:

- employee records
- worker availability
- qualifications
- training
- credentials
- document expiry
- timesheets
- payroll information
- supervision
- leave
- workforce reporting

For organisations operating under the SCHADS Award, workforce processes may involve additional complexity.

When comparing software, ask exactly what the system manages and what still needs to be handled externally.

Do not rely solely on broad terms such as “SCHADS compliant”.

Your organisation remains responsible for applying the relevant employment requirements correctly.

## 10. Can we control who has access to information?

Not everyone in an organisation should necessarily have access to everything.

A support worker, finance team member, service manager and executive may all need different levels of access.

Ask whether the platform supports:

- role-based permissions
- restricted participant information
- administrative permissions
- manager access
- user deactivation
- access history
- audit trails

Also ask what happens when a staff member leaves the organisation.

Can their access be removed immediately without affecting the records they previously created?

This is particularly important when dealing with sensitive participant and workforce information.

## 11. What does implementation actually involve?

Software selection does not finish when the contract is signed.

Implementation can have a major impact on whether the new system succeeds.

Ask potential software providers about:

- data migration
- participant migration
- worker migration
- document migration
- system configuration
- integrations
- training
- testing
- onboarding support
- expected internal responsibilities
- post-launch support

Also ask who will own the implementation inside your organisation.

A technically capable platform can still fail if staff do not understand how it should be used.

## 12. What is the real cost of the software?

Do not compare platforms only by the number displayed on the pricing page.

The true cost may include:

- monthly subscription
- per-user fees
- per-participant fees
- implementation
- onboarding
- data migration
- integrations
- additional modules
- training
- storage
- support
- future upgrades

It is also worth considering the internal cost of operating the system.

A cheaper platform can become expensive if staff spend significant time:

- entering the same information multiple times
- exporting spreadsheets
- manually reconciling data
- moving between systems
- correcting avoidable errors

When comparing NDIS software, look at the total operational cost rather than subscription price alone.

## A simple NDIS software comparison checklist

Before making a final decision, ask whether the platform can demonstrate the following using your organisation's actual workflows:

- Participant intake and records
- Service agreements and funding
- Rostering and workforce management
- Progress notes and documentation
- Incident management
- Timesheets
- Payroll workflow
- Invoicing
- NDIS claiming
- Reconciliation
- Compliance evidence
- Reporting
- User permissions
- Data migration
- Implementation and training

If possible, involve people from different parts of the organisation in the evaluation.

Operations may notice problems finance does not.

Support workers may identify usability issues management misses.

Compliance teams may ask questions that were not considered during the initial demonstration.

## All-in-one platform or multiple systems?

There is no universal answer.

Some providers prefer separate specialist systems for areas such as:

- rostering
- payroll
- accounting
- participant management
- compliance

Others prefer a more connected environment where operational information can move through the organisation with fewer handovers.

The key question is not whether one approach is automatically better.

It is:

**How much work is required to keep all of your systems connected?**

If your team constantly needs to export, import, reconcile or manually re-enter information, it may be worth reviewing whether the current setup still makes sense.

## Small NDIS provider vs growing organisation

The right software will also depend on your size and stage.

A smaller provider may prioritise:

- simplicity
- affordable pricing
- participant records
- basic rostering
- progress notes
- incidents
- invoicing
- easy onboarding

A growing organisation may increasingly require:

- approval workflows
- stronger workforce controls
- multiple teams or locations
- role-based access
- advanced reporting
- integrations
- automated workflows
- deeper audit trails
- greater operational visibility

Do not buy unnecessary complexity.

But also consider whether the system can continue supporting the organisation as requirements change.

After every NDIS software demonstration, ask your team:

**“Did this platform actually make our work simpler?”**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I compare when choosing NDIS software?

Compare the complete operational workflow, including participant management, rostering, documentation, workforce management, invoicing, claiming, compliance, reporting, security, implementation and total cost.

There is no single platform that is best for every provider. The right choice depends on your organisation's size, service model, workforce, operational complexity, existing systems and future requirements.

Not necessarily. Small providers should focus first on the operational problems they genuinely need to solve. However, it is worth considering whether the system can support the organisation if participant numbers, employees and compliance requirements increase.

Use real scenarios from your organisation. Ask the provider to demonstrate a complete workflow rather than only showing dashboards and individual features.
