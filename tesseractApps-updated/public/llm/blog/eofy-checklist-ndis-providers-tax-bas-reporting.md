# EOFY Checklist for NDIS Providers: Tax, BAS & Reporting Made Simple

A step-by-step EOFY guide for Australian NDIS providers covering STP finalisation, BAS, super guarantee reconciliation, NDIS claim reconciliation.

End of financial year lands at an awkward time for NDIS providers. It is the same window as the new NDIS Pricing Schedule taking effect, [SCHADS Award rate changes](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-2026-the-complete-guide-for-ndis-and-disability-providers) flowing through payroll, and the usual STP finalisation, BAS and superannuation obligations every employer faces. None of these processes are optional, and several depend on each other: your STP finalisation only reconciles cleanly if your payroll data is correct, and your payroll data is only correct if your award rates and NDIS claim rates were updated on time.

That is what makes EOFY for NDIS providers a genuinely different task to a standard close-out, and why a generic checklist usually leaves gaps. This checklist works through each obligation in order, with the specific dates, rates and thresholds that apply for the FY2025-26 close-out, so your finance team can work through it once rather than chasing each requirement separately.

**Key FY2025-26 dates at a glance**

- **Q4 super guarantee payment:** 28 July
- **STP finalisation (arms-length employees):** 14 July
- **Payroll tax annual reconciliation:** Around 21 July (confirm your state)
- **Monthly BAS (with registered agent extension):** Varies by lodgment program tier

## 1. Single Touch Payroll finalisation

STP finalisation is the declaration confirming your payroll reporting for the financial year is complete for every employee. Until it is lodged, employees see "not tax ready" in myGov and cannot accurately lodge their own returns.

- **Deadline:** 14 July, for arms-length employees. Closely held payees, such as family members of a director, may have a later due date, so check this separately rather than applying one deadline to your whole workforce.
- **Before you finalise:** Confirm you are finalising the correct financial year, that you have reported under the right ABN throughout, and that if you changed payroll software or employee payroll IDs during the year, those changes have been reconciled rather than left as duplicate or orphaned records.
- **Fringe benefits:** If you provided any fringe benefits during the FBT year (1 April to 31 March), the reportable fringe benefits amount needs to be included through STP as part of finalisation, not lodged separately.
- **STP Phase 2 detail:** Confirm income types are correctly disaggregated: salary, overtime, bonuses, leave and commissions reported separately rather than bundled, along with employment basis and cessation reason for any employee who left during the year. Errors here are a common reason finalisation gets rejected or queried.

For a disability provider running a large casual and part-time SCHADS-covered workforce, EOFY payroll for disability services is also the point where sleepover, [broken shift ](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-broken-shifts-rostering-without-breaches)and [penalty rate](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-overtime-penalty-rates) misclassifications from earlier in the year surface. If your STP totals look wrong for a pay category, check it against the award rules that actually applied on those pay dates before finalising, not after.

## 2. Superannuation guarantee reconciliation

The Superannuation Guarantee rate for FY2025-26 is 12%, up from 11.5% the previous year, and this is the final scheduled increase under the legislated timetable. It applies to all wages paid on or after 1 July 2025, even where part of the pay period falls before that date.

- **Concessional contributions cap:** $30,000 for FY2025-26, up from $27,500. This includes employer SG contributions and any salary sacrifice, relevant if you are advising employees or checking your own contribution strategy.
- **Maximum contribution base:** $62,500 per quarter for FY2025-26. Super is not payable on earnings above this cap in a given quarter, which matters for higher-paid clinical or management roles.
- **Missed or late payments:** The super guarantee charge applies if contributions are late or short, carrying 10% annual interest plus a $20 administration fee per employee. Unlike an on-time SG payment, a late SGC payment is not tax deductible. If you find a shortfall during EOFY reconciliation, lodge a Superannuation Guarantee Charge Statement and pay it promptly rather than folding it quietly into next quarter's payment.

**A structural change to flag now, not just at EOFY:** Payday Super began on 1 July 2026, requiring SG to be paid at the same time as wages rather than quarterly. The Small Business Superannuation Clearing House also closed permanently to all users from that date. If your payroll process still assumes a quarterly super cycle, this needs to be corrected as a process change, not an EOFY line item, since it affects every pay run going forward, not just your FY2025-26 close-out.

## 3. BAS and GST reconciliation

BAS for an NDIS provider carries one wrinkle most generic small business guidance skips: most support items are GST-free, but not all provider income is, so getting this wrong in either direction is common. Whether you lodge monthly or quarterly, EOFY is the point to check your full-year BAS position rather than just lodging the final period in isolation.

- **Reconcile GST collected and claimed** for the full year against your general ledger, checking specifically for any NDIS-related supply that was coded incorrectly.
- **Match PAYG withholding** reported through STP against what was actually remitted to the ATO across the year, catching any timing mismatches before they show up as a discrepancy at tax return time.
- **Check your lodgment program dates.** Monthly BAS reporters using a registered agent typically get a short extension beyond the standard 21st of the month deadline, but the exact extension depends on your agent's lodgment program tier, so confirm the specific date with your agent rather than assuming a standard extension applies.

## 4. Payroll tax reconciliation

If your total Australian wages exceed your state's payroll tax threshold, an annual reconciliation is due to your state revenue office, separate from your BAS and STP obligations.

- Confirm actual wages match what was declared in your periodic returns across the year.
- Most states set the annual reconciliation due date around 21 July, but this varies, so check your specific state's date rather than assuming national consistency.
- If you operate across multiple states, which is common for larger NDIS providers, work through each state's threshold and wage base separately rather than one combined total.

## 5. NDIS-specific claim reconciliation

This is the step that is easy to miss if your EOFY checklist was built for a generic services business rather than an NDIS provider specifically.

- **Reconcile claims against the NDIS portal,** not just your own billing system, to catch any [claims rejected or adjusted](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/ndis-bulk-payment-claim-rejections) by the Agency during the year that did not flow back into your internal records.
- **Check the [1 July pricing transition ](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/ndis-pricing-arrangements-2026-27-what-changed)specifically.** Any pay period or service delivered either side of 1 July needs to be claimed at the price limits that applied on the actual service date, not the date the claim was processed. This is one of the most common sources of NDIS claim errors precisely because it lands in the same week as several other EOFY processes competing for your team's attention.
- **Confirm [SIL claims](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/ndis-sil-mandatory-registration-2026-checklist/)** used the correct item codes either side of the transition, since SIL item numbers changed prefix this year. A claim using the wrong code is not just a data error; it can be rejected outright.
- **Reconfirm TTP loading eligibility** if your organisation claims it, since ongoing eligibility depends on meeting benchmarking requirements that should be checked annually, not assumed to carry over unchanged.

## 6. Audit-ready records, not just EOFY records

Everything above produces records the [Fair Work Ombudsman](https://tesseractapps.com.au/blog/schads-award-audit-guide-self-assess-fair-work), the NDIA or the ATO can request, sometimes years later. Under the Fair Work Act, employee records must be kept for seven years, whether or not your EOFY process currently treats record-keeping as a compliance task or a filing afterthought. Every reconciliation you complete this week creates evidence. How you store it determines whether that evidence works for you or against you.

- Store STP finalisation confirmations, BAS lodgment receipts, super payment confirmations and NDIS claim reconciliation reports together by financial year, not scattered across separate systems.
- Keep a short written record of any correction made during EOFY reconciliation: what was wrong, when it was fixed, and how. This is exactly the evidence that helps if a regulator asks about a specific pay period later.
- Treat this year's reconciliation as the baseline for next year's opening figures, so errors do not compound silently from one financial year into the next.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the STP finalisation deadline for FY2025-26?

14 July, for arms-length employees. Closely held payees, such as directors' family members paid informally, may have a later due date, and this should be checked separately rather than assumed to match the general deadline.

### Do NDIS providers have different EOFY obligations to other employers?

The core obligations, STP, super, BAS and payroll tax, are the same as any employer. What is different is the overlap with NDIS-specific processes landing in the same window: the 1 July Pricing Schedule transition, SCHADS Award rate changes, and claim reconciliation against the NDIA portal, all of which can affect the payroll and revenue figures feeding into your other EOFY reporting.

### What happens if we miss a superannuation guarantee payment?

You need to lodge a Superannuation Guarantee Charge Statement and pay the shortfall, which carries 10% annual interest plus a $20 administration fee per affected employee. Unlike an on-time SG payment, a late SGC payment is not tax deductible, which makes catching a shortfall during EOFY reconciliation rather than at your next external audit worth the extra checking time.

### Does Payday Super affect our FY2025-26 close-out?

Not directly, since it began on 1 July 2026, after the FY2025-26 year closed. It does affect how you process super from that date forward, so it belongs on your EOFY checklist as a forward-looking action item: confirm your payroll system pays SG each payday rather than quarterly, and confirm your super clearing arrangement no longer relies on the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House, which closed permanently from the same date.
