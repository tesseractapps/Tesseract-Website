# Broken Shifts Under SCHADS: How to Roster Without Breaching the Award?

How the broken-shift clause works under SCHADS, when allowances apply, and rostering patterns AU providers use to stay compliant?

Broken shifts are one of those SCHADS provisions that everyone in rostering knows about and almost no-one fully understands. The allowance is small enough that providers sometimes ignore it, and the eligibility rules are specific enough that applying it to the wrong employees creates a different kind of problem. Between under-applying it, over-applying it, and rostering past the 12-hour span without noticing, broken shifts generate more avoidable compliance exposure than almost anything else in the award.

This guide covers who the broken shift rules apply to, how the allowance works, what the [rostering ](https://tesseractapps.com.au/capabilities/rostering-scheduling)constraints actually are, and how to build compliant split-day patterns around the way NDIS supports are delivered.

## What is a broken shift under SCHADS? 

Under clause 25.6 of the SCHADS Award, a broken shift is a shift worked in two or more separate periods of work within a single day, with an unpaid break in between that is not a meal break. The distinction matters. A 30-minute unpaid lunch in the middle of a continuous shift is not a broken shift. A three-hour unpaid gap between a morning and an afternoon block is.

This is the pattern that shows up constantly in NDIS community access rosters: a worker assists with morning personal care from 7am to 10am, is off duty and unpaid, then returns for evening support from 5pm to 8pm. That is a broken shift.

A broken shift can include up to three periods of work and two unpaid breaks, but the second break requires the employee's written agreement before the shift or as part of an agreed regular pattern. No agreement, no second break. Rostering one without it is a breach.

## Who the broken shift clause actually covers?

This is the detail most providers miss. Broken shifts under SCHADS apply only to home care employees providing services in clients' homes, and social and community services employees performing disability services work. Not all SACS employees. [Support coordinators](https://tesseractapps.com.au/solutions/support-coordination) and case managers doing non-disability work are not eligible.

The allowance also applies only to full-time and part-time employees. Casuals are excluded. Their 25% loading compensates for fragmented work patterns. Paying the allowance to a casual is an overpayment. Not paying it to an eligible permanent employee is an underpayment. Both directions create exposure.

## The 12-hour span rule: where most roster errors happen 

The total time between the start of the first work period and the end of the last must not exceed 12 hours. Any work performed beyond that span is paid at double time, regardless of how many hours the employee actually worked that day.

This is what catches providers out. A worker who starts at 8am, takes a four-hour unpaid break, and finishes at 9pm has a 13-hour span. The final hour attracts double time even if total hours worked is only eight. The trigger is the span, not the hours. Providers who roster around shift length without tracking the span will breach this regularly.

Employees working broken shifts on consecutive days must also receive a minimum 10-hour break between shifts. Eight hours if the preceding shift included a sleepover.

## How the broken shift allowance works?

The allowance compensates for the inconvenience of fragmented work and sits on top of, not instead of, the applicable hourly and penalty rates. A broken shift on a Saturday attracts both the Saturday penalty on the hours worked and the broken shift allowance. They are independent entitlements.

From 1 July 2025, the amounts are:

- $20.82 per shift for one unpaid break
- $27.56 per shift for two unpaid breaks (written employee agreement required)

These are per shift per day, not per break. Both amounts are indexed annually alongside base rates and will update from 1 July 2026. Each work period within the broken shift must also meet the two-hour minimum engagement rule. A worker cannot be brought in for 45 minutes, stood down, and called back. Each block is a minimum of two hours.

## A practical rostering example 

A support worker delivering NDIS community access works two blocks: 7am to 9am and 5pm to 8pm. The span from first start to last finish is 13 hours, which breaches the 12-hour limit. The final hour would attract double time.

The fix is straightforward: shift the morning block to 8am to 10am, bringing the span to exactly 12 hours. A 7am start with a 7pm finish also works if the evening block ends earlier. If that same worker is permanent and delivering disability support services, the $20.82 broken shift allowance applies on each day the pattern runs. If they are casual, it does not, regardless of how fragmented the day looks. And if the pattern runs on consecutive days, the gap between 8pm on day one and 8am on day two is exactly 12 hours, right on the minimum. A 9pm finish would drop that rest period to 11 hours, still compliant. A 10pm finish gets it to 10 hours, the floor.

## Common mistakes NDIS providers make 

Applying the allowance to all SCHADS staff. The broken shift rules do not cover everyone under the award. Paying it to ineligible roles is an overpayment that is awkward to unwind.

Confusing meal breaks with broken shift gaps. A standard unpaid lunch does not trigger the clause. The worker must be genuinely released from duty for an extended unpaid period between separate engagements.

Tracking hours instead of span. A worker doing six hours across a 13-hour span has still breached the award. Double time applies to the final hour regardless of actual hours worked.

Missing the allowance entirely. It does not appear automatically in most payroll systems unless it has been configured to trigger. Many providers simply do not know it exists until an audit surfaces it.

### Broken shifts under SCHADS FAQ 

Who is eligible for the broken shift allowance? 

Full-time and part-time home care employees, and SACS employees performing disability services work. Casual employees are excluded.

What is the broken shift allowance in 2026? 

$20.82 per shift for one unpaid break, $27.56 for two (with written employee agreement). Both update with the annual wage review from 1 July 2026.

What is the maximum span for a broken shift? 

12 hours from the start of the first work period to the end of the last. Hours beyond that are paid at double time.

Does a meal break count as a broken shift? 

No. A standard unpaid meal break during a continuous shift does not trigger the clause.

How much rest is required between broken shifts on consecutive days?

A minimum of 10 hours. Eight hours if the preceding shift included a sleepover.
